## What we need to learn

A lake can appear in several tiles, sometimes with different coordinate systems.
We need to assemble its records without losing coverage, mixing acquisitions, or dropping the provenance of overlapping observations.
We also need to measure whether processing by lake or by tile changes the work required.

The raster-mask hypothesis is fewer requests with tile-first workers, because neighboring lakes can share open files and decoded blocks.
That expectation does not extend automatically to the lazy stack. Stage 3's shared graph repeatedly read chunks, sometimes increasing both requests and bytes.
These expectations come from [measurement findings 19 and 21](../measurements.md). Their size remains to be measured across tiles.

The new information is:

- Each anchor's requested bytes per additional tile, beside the polygon and buffered-support shares that tile contributes.
- Support observed by two or more tiles of the same datatake, counted once as overlapping area.
- Support left uncovered after the tile union and after the product-footprint union.
- Complete-workload cost for both work organizations, including cases where the lazy reader loses the raster reader's sharing benefit.

Poor raster sharing would weaken the case for tile batching in this workload.
Large lazy read amplification would motivate a separate tuning experiment before choosing that reader.
Unexpected uncovered support would require revisiting selection before claiming complete lake extraction.
High costs for small additional shares inform processing estimates, without authorizing the removal of those tile observations.

The [owner's prototype structure](2026-09-14-prototype-structure-and-stage-1.md) places this workload before the scientific comparisons in Stage 5.
Stage 4 assembles records while preserving native grids and separate tile values.
It does not blend pixels, resample imagery, or decide which overlapping value is scientifically preferable.
The [draft contract](../data-contract.md) and [existing decisions](../decisions/0003-earth-search-2021-first-gap-survey-cross-tile.md) remain the constraints.

## Establish the inputs before timing

Stage 3's greedy selection stops when every lake is placed. Stage 4 must enumerate all relevant tiles instead.

1. Search the buffered region extent within Stage 3's 2025-06-01 to 2025-10-31 window.
2. Enumerate tile intersections with each polygon and its near-land support, including tiles that contain only part of either.
3. Record rejected candidates, missing assets, and uncertain coverage explicitly.
4. Select one common acquisition group per region, then freeze its items, bands, memberships, and input digests.
5. Print the planned worker count and estimated requested bytes before any imagery read.

Estimate windowed traffic from the reviewed Stage 4 workload rates, with reference hashes and reader settings recorded.
Scale rates by lake-item memberships and repetitions. Report incompatible evidence as unknown, rather than substituting whole-file sizes.
The estimate assumes comparable polygon sizes, density, and compression. It is not a hard transfer guard.

Group by the complete `s2:datatake_id`, checking platform consistency and retaining every original sensing time.
No orbit field is required. Missing datatake identity is reported as ungrouped, rather than inferred from a calendar date.
Tile timestamps need not be identical within a group.

For each tile and declared datastrip within a group, retain candidates with the requested assets and supported native grids.
Choose the latest creation time, then the smaller item id. Missing creation time sorts last.
Keep distinct datastrips as separate products. Missing datastrip identity prevents deduplication.
Save discarded alternatives and reasons, including processing versions.
An unusable or absent tile remains in the missing-tile report.

A group is eligible when its usable tiles' extent union covers every lake's buffered support within the declared tolerance.
Catalog geometry and item footprints must be available for scoring. Unknown geometry is reported and excludes the group from automatic selection.
Rank eligible groups by this ordered list:

1. Minimize the largest uncovered buffered-support fraction after footprint unions, calculated separately for each lake.
2. Minimize the highest item cloud percentage in the group. Unknown cloud cover sorts last.
3. Prefer the latest datatake, using its earliest recorded sensing time.
4. Break remaining ties by the smaller datatake id, then the sorted item-id list.

The strict per-member footprint preference used in version 2 is removed after the full-run review.
Footprint unions remain coarse ranking hints. They do not establish pixel validity or impose a hard membership filter.
Retain native tile candidates and each product's separate footprint diagnostics.

For ranking, coverage within 1e-6 counts as complete. Save the unrounded fractions and every rejected group's score or exclusion reason.
A spatial candidate absent from this datatake is recorded as unavailable for that group, even when other tiles cover the same area.
Complete geometric support and a complete candidate-tile set are separate results.
Expected extraction contributions come from the frozen group's available items. Unavailable candidates remain visible alongside them.
Both paths must account for every expected contribution, including an asset failure discovered later.

The saved Lanier selections come from different datatakes and dates. They cannot become one observation merely by concatenation.
If no common group covers the proposed workload, report that limitation and revise the case selection before pixel reads.
Do not silently substitute a different date for one missing tile.

Report three separate notions of coverage:

- Geometry: polygon and buffered support covered by the union of tile extents.
- Product footprint: that support covered by the chosen items' footprint union.
- Observed data: selected pixels present or marked no-data, by tile, band, resolution, and class.

Use one declared metric reference CRS per lake for every diagnostic area, including the buffered-share scores.
Densify tile edges in their native CRS before transforming them. Apply equivalent checks to long footprint and polygon segments.
Refine segmentation until successive area and overlap results converge within the declared tolerance, then record the spacing and observed change.
This tests numerical refinement, rather than error against exact geometry or source footprint accuracy.
Also check the polygon's round trip. A passing round trip alone does not establish adequate edge approximation.
Do not assume all pilot boundaries have sufficiently dense vertices.
Compute union area, uncovered area, and the union of pairwise intersections. The latter counts multiply observed ground only once.
Summed tile percentages double-count overlaps and cannot prove coverage.
Native tile grids still govern extraction and the existing pixel-center near-land rule.
Buffer intersection is a candidate test. A candidate with no selected pixel remains a valid, explicitly recorded outcome.
17RML has positive polygon overlap in the saved candidate table. Treat it as partial water coverage pending preparation, rather than a near-land-only example.

## Compare two ways to organize the same work

| Path | Worker unit | Output assembly |
|---|---|---|
| Lake first | One lake and acquisition group. Read its selected windows from every relevant tile, sequentially | Order the lake's contribution records by their canonical keys |
| Tile first | One item, retaining separate workers for different datastrips of the same tile. Share file access across selected lakes | Group contribution records by lake and acquisition, then order their keys |

Both paths use the same frozen inputs and Stage 3's contribution records.
Rasterio keeps each file open across its lakes. The lazy stack retains Stage 3's shared graph and separate lake computations.
Count observed opens for both readers. A shared graph does not imply one file open or retained decoded chunks.
Their process boundaries intentionally differ. The comparison includes startup and cache effects of those work units.
It does not isolate loop order alone.

Every lazy graph holds exactly one item on that item's native grid, or an aligned window of that grid.
Specify the GeoBox explicitly at the band's native resolution and verify the returned grid.
Reject any request that combines items or changes CRS, resolution, or grid alignment.
Two items in different CRSs must produce separate graphs. A fixture must demonstrate that a merge request is refused before loading.

A contribution key includes lake, polygon version, acquisition group, tile, item, band, and resolution, with grid metadata bound to the record.
Assembly is its canonical key ordering, with each record's existing value digest, pixel-set digest, window, counts, and missing status.
Compare ordered keys and each contribution's digests, preserving the association between pixel order and value order.
Reject duplicate keys, including repeated contributions after a retry. Report conflicting records explicitly.
Do not write extracted pixel fragments or time a storage format in this pass.
This verifies extraction and logical grouping. It does not test stored-pixel readback or physical concatenation cost.

Equal row and column numbers in different tiles never identify the same record.
Different tiles' overlapping observations remain separate.
One missing contribution makes the assembled observation incomplete, rather than shrinking its expected membership list.

### Primary-tile labels

Assumption A22 remains provisional. Its edge-distance wording does not define the same metric as largest buffered share.
Recommended replacement for owner consideration: choose the spatial candidate containing the largest share of the lake's buffered polygon.
Treat shares within 1e-6 of the largest share as tied, then choose the smaller tile id.
Freeze that choice across the experiment's datatakes. Label its contributions primary and every other tile's contributions overlap.
If a group lacks that primary tile, report it missing instead of silently promoting another tile.
Preserve all contributions regardless of role.

This is a proposed revision, not an interpretation already authorized by A22.
Until the owner resolves it, ambiguous roles remain unresolved in benchmark accounting and cannot be presented as satisfying the contract's role field.
The extraction and digest comparison can still be checked independently of that label.
No change to A22 or the contract is made in this planning revision.
Calculating a separate deepest-interior-point metric is deferred. It is another possible rule, not the uniquely literal meaning of A22.

## Acceptance checks

| Check | Evidence required |
|---|---|
| Complete accounting | Every planned lake, tile, item, band, method, and repetition appears as measured, empty, missing, or failed |
| Spatial coverage | Tile and footprint unions identify uncovered support. Report any remainder instead of claiming complete coverage |
| Equivalent paths | Identical ordered contribution keys, counts, value digests, and pixel-set digests for each method across both paths |
| Equivalent readers | Raster mask and lazy stack agree on values, native pixel identities, and classes within every contribution |
| Native grids preserved | One item per lazy graph. Different CRSs and shifted grids remain distinct, and attempted merges are rejected |
| Repeatable assembly | Shuffled completion produces identical ordered records. Repeated keys and conflicting records are rejected |
| Missing inputs | Missing tiles, unresolved bands, no-data values, and valid empty selections remain distinguishable |

Reuse the existing coverage tolerance, 1e-6 relative area, for declared geometry checks.
Confirm transformation accuracy against that tolerance before interpreting a cross-CRS union.
Pixel values and identities have zero tolerance within equivalent selections. Timing has no correctness tolerance.
Between different tiles, equal values are not an acceptance requirement. Their scientific agreement belongs to Stage 5.

Fixtures must include partial coverage, full overlap, near-land-only membership, shifted grids, different CRSs, and acquisitions that cannot be paired.
Reuse Stage 3's fixtures and helpers where practical. Add tests for the cross-tile behavior rather than duplicating existing reader tests.
Historical Stage 3 digests are a baseline only where the item, grid, band, geometry, and method family match.
