I-01
Cloud dilation flags whole ponds		Since baseline 04.00 the scene classification dilates clouds by 80 m, cloud shadows by 40 m, and snow by 20 m. Only clouds with probability above 65 percent and larger than three pixels are dilated. Dilation is suppressed in shoreline regions and at soil and snow boundaries. Over urban areas only clouds above 1000 pixels are dilated. The layer is produced at 20 m, so a qualifying cloud within four cells can flag a 10 m pond. Products before baseline 04.00 are not dilated.For small ponds the cloud class can mean a qualifying cloud within 80 m rather than cloud over the pixel. The expectation, unmeasured, is that usable scenes fall faster for small ponds than for lakes. Measure it on the pilot set. How ESA defines shoreline regions is not on the pages read. Semantics change at baseline 04.00 on any archive that mixes original and reprocessed products. The store keeps the class code and the cloud probability so the rule can be revisited.open

Cross-tile mosaicking is a MAJOR CONCERN
MAJOR CONCERN		Adjacent tiles are processed separately and differ in classification and reflectance in their overlap. Tiles in different UTM zones have different pixel grids. A water body in an overlap or on a zone boundary has two sets of values per date. One near-land buffer can span tiles. Tools that stitch or fuse tiles hide all of this.MAJOR CONCERN. The owner requires a thorough investigation of cross-tile mosaicking before any stitched value is trusted. The store never mosaics. It keeps every tile's values with a primary marker under the provisional rule of assumption A22. The prototype measures differences between tiles over the same pixels on the same dates, per band and per quality layer. No consumer-facing mosaic is specified before that measurement.open
